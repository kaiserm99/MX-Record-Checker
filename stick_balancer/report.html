Stick Balancer Lab

An agent that teaches itself to balance a stick on a cart, then a stick with one joint, then two. Same code, one number changes.

Watch the trained agents

Deterministic policy, one 20-second episode (1000 control steps at 50 Hz). The cart is 1 kg, the whole stick 0.1 kg and 1 m regardless of how many joints it has, and it moves through air on real bearings (see the physics below). Fails if the cart leaves ±2.4 m or any link leans past its limit.

Return is one point per surviving step minus small penalties, so 1000 is a perfect 20-second balance. Training stops as soon as the evaluation mean clears 980.

How it works

The physics (physics.py)

Coordinates are the cart position and the absolute angle of each link from vertical. Every link is a uniform rod. The equations of motion come straight from d'Alembert's principle and are solved numerically each step, so the same code handles one link or ten:

M(q)·q̈ = u·e₀ + Σᵢ Jᵢᵀ( Fᵍʳᵃᵛᵢ − mᵢ·J̇ᵢ·q̇ ) + f_diss(q, q̇)
M(q) = m₀e₀e₀ᵀ + Σᵢ ( mᵢ JᵢᵀJᵢ + Iᵢ eᵢeᵢᵀ )

Jᵢ is the Jacobian of link i's centre of mass. Integration is classic RK4 with 5 ms sub-steps. With one link and dissipation off, the accelerations match Gymnasium's CartPole-v1 formulas to 1e-10, and energy is conserved to 1e-4 over 3 s (test_physics.py).

Dissipation, f_diss. The realistic preset (the default) adds what a real stick in a real room feels:

  • Air drag on every link: pressure drag on a cylinder in cross-flow, ½ρ·C_d·d·|vₙ|vₙ per metre, integrated along the rod with 3-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature because the tip moves faster than the hinge. ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, C_d = 1.1, d = 2 cm.
  • Air drag on the cart: ½ρ·(C_d A)·|ẋ|ẋ with C_d A = 0.01 m².
  • Bearing friction in each hinge: viscous 0.002 N·m·s/rad (the value Glück et al. measured on their triple pendulum) plus 1e-4 N·m Coulomb, acting on the relative joint velocity with the reaction on the link below.
  • Rail friction on the cart: Coulomb μ = 0.01 of the total weight, plus optional viscous drag.

Coulomb sign() terms are smoothed with tanh(v/1 cm·s⁻¹) so RK4 stays well-behaved. Tests check the rod-drag torque against the closed form −k·ω|ω|·l⁴/4 and that mechanical energy never increases. A 1 m, 100 g stick swinging freely halves its amplitude in about 7 s; pass physics="ideal" for the frictionless textbook model.

The task (env.py)

Observation · x, ẋ, θ₁…θ_N, θ̇₁…θ̇_N (raw; VecNormalize learns the scaling).

Action · one number in [−1, 1], scaled to a horizontal force on the cart.

Reward · 1 per step, minus 0.05·(x/x_max)², minus 0.05·mean(θᵢ/θ_max)², minus 0.001·a². Zero on the step that fails.

Episode · ends when the cart leaves the track or a link leans past θ_max; truncated at 1000 steps.

Start · every coordinate and velocity uniform in ±0.05, like CartPole-v1.

The recipes (recipes.py)

PPO from Stable-Baselines3 with observation and reward normalisation, 4 subprocess environments, linearly decaying learning rate and clip range. Numbers descend from the tuned rl-baselines3-zoo entries for CartPole-v1 and InvertedDoublePendulum. SAC is wired in too (--algo sac) with the zoo's PyBullet double-pendulum settings.

What the research says

Algorithm choice

  • PPO is the standard first choice: the rl-baselines3-zoo solves CartPole with it in 1e5 steps and MuJoCo's InvertedDoublePendulum in about 1e6, with observation normalisation. It parallelises well and is what we run here.
  • SAC, TD3, TQC are the state of the art for single-process continuous control (Stable-Baselines3's own guidance). The SAC expert on InvertedDoublePendulum-v5 scores 9356 ± 0.06, while PPO runs on the same task span 1507 to 9358 across seeds. Every real-hardware triple and quadruple pendulum result uses an off-policy method (TQC, actor-critic with virtual experience replay).
  • DQN only fits the discrete-action CartPole and does not scale to the force control a multi-joint stick needs.
  • Budgets seen in the literature: CartPole 5e4–1e5; single continuous pendulum ≤ 3e5; double pendulum 5e5 (SAC) to 2e6 (PPO); cart-pole swing-up with domain randomisation 3e6. Plan on 1e6–3e6 more per extra link.

Why more joints is so much harder

  • The upright equilibrium of an N-link pendulum has N unstable modes. With CartPole-like parameters the fastest one doubles a disturbance every 149 ms for 1 link, 83 ms for 2, 62 ms for 3, 51 ms for 4.
  • Åström & Murray's rule for an unstable pole p is a loop crossover above ~1.7p, so the required control bandwidth climbs from about 8 rad/s (1 link) to 19 rad/s (3 links). Shorter, lighter links push it higher still; a 20 Hz controller is comfortable for two links and marginal for three.
  • The region of attraction shrinks with N ("larger n needs smaller perturbations to fall"), so random exploration ends episodes almost immediately and the alive bonus has to carry early learning. Bounded cart force and a finite track cap what is recoverable at all.
  • Absolute link angles simulate faster and more accurately than relative joint angles (Serkibayev et al. 2025); we use absolute angles.

Practices we adopted

  • Normalise observations and rewards (VecNormalize) and save the statistics with the model; every zoo continuous-control entry does this.
  • Alive bonus plus small quadratic costs, the shape of MuJoCo's InvertedDoublePendulum reward, so the return stays monotone in survival time. Pure quadratic costs make early failure attractive.
  • Truncate, don't terminate, at the time limit, and pay no bonus on the failing step (the Gymnasium v5 bug fix).
  • RK4 with 5 ms sub-steps: the MuJoCo reference envs integrate with RK4 at 10 ms and frame-skip 2–5; explicit Euler at 20 ms gains energy on jointed sticks.
  • Symmetric [−1, 1] actions scaled to a force inside the env, a small u² penalty, and deterministic evaluation with a stop threshold.
  • Realistic dissipation: air drag and bearing friction, with the hinge damping taken from measured hardware, so a policy is not trained on a vacuum.

Next steps if you want to go further

  • Swing-up: start the links hanging down, feed sin/cos of the angles, use dm_control's product-of-tolerances reward or the MuJoCo tip-height reward, no failure termination.
  • Curriculum: no paper grows one policy from 1 to N joints (the observation size changes). What works is task curricula (balance → swing-up), parameter curricula (ramp initial noise or drag), and growing a short, light top link toward its target size.
  • Domain randomisation of the parameters the loop is most sensitive to (masses, hinge damping, actuator gain) with a linear ramp, plus an action low-pass filter, if the policy should ever drive a real cart.
  • Compare with MuJoCo: InvertedDoublePendulum-v5 or dm_control's three_poles for published baselines.
