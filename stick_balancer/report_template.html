Stick Balancer Lab

An agent that teaches itself to balance a stick on a cart, then a stick with one joint, then two. Same code, one number changes.

Watch the trained agents

Deterministic policy, one 20-second episode (1000 control steps at 50 Hz). The cart is 1 kg, the whole stick 0.1 kg and 1 m regardless of how many joints it has. Fails if the cart leaves ±2.4 m or any link leans past its limit.

Return is one point per surviving step minus small penalties, so 1000 is a perfect 20-second balance. Training stops as soon as the evaluation mean clears 980.

How it works

The physics (physics.py)

Coordinates are the cart position and the absolute angle of each link from vertical. Every link is a uniform rod. The equations of motion come straight from d'Alembert's principle and are solved numerically each step, so the same 60 lines handle one link or ten:

M(q)·q̈ = u·e₀ + Σᵢ Jᵢᵀ( Fᵍʳᵃᵛᵢ − mᵢ·J̇ᵢ·q̇ ) − damping
M(q) = m₀e₀e₀ᵀ + Σᵢ ( mᵢ JᵢᵀJᵢ + Iᵢ eᵢeᵢᵀ )

Jᵢ is the Jacobian of link i's centre of mass. Integration is classic RK4 with 5 ms sub-steps. With one link the accelerations match Gymnasium's CartPole-v1 formulas to 1e-10, and energy is conserved to 1e-4 over 3 s with no forcing (test_physics.py).

The task (env.py)

Observation · x, ẋ, θ₁…θ_N, θ̇₁…θ̇_N (raw; VecNormalize learns the scaling).

Action · one number in [−1, 1], scaled to a horizontal force on the cart.

Reward · 1 per step, minus 0.05·(x/x_max)², minus 0.05·mean(θᵢ/θ_max)², minus 0.001·a². Zero on the step that fails.

Episode · ends when the cart leaves the track or a link leans past θ_max; truncated at 1000 steps.

Start · every coordinate and velocity uniform in ±0.05, like CartPole-v1.

The recipes (recipes.py)

PPO from Stable-Baselines3 with observation and reward normalisation, 4 subprocess environments, linearly decaying learning rate and clip range. Numbers descend from the tuned rl-baselines3-zoo entries for CartPole-v1 and InvertedDoublePendulum. SAC is wired in too (--algo sac) with the zoo's PyBullet double-pendulum settings.

What the research says

Algorithm choice

  • PPO is the default for these tasks: simple, stable, cheap per step, and the zoo's tuned configs solve CartPole in 1e5 steps and the MuJoCo double pendulum in about 1e6. It is what we run first.
  • SAC / TD3 are far more sample-efficient (they reuse every transition many times) and often solve InvertedDoublePendulum in a few 10⁴ environment steps, at the price of slower wall-clock per step. Use SAC when simulation is expensive, PPO when it is cheap.
  • DQN only fits the discrete-action CartPole; it does not scale to the force control a multi-joint stick needs.

Why more joints is so much harder

  • The upright equilibrium of an N-link pendulum has N unstable eigenvalues, and the fastest one grows with the link count and shrinks with link length. Short, light upper links diverge in well under 100 ms.
  • A single force on the cart must stabilise all N angles at once (underactuated by N), so the controller needs both precise velocity estimates and high control bandwidth. Our recipes raise the force limit and the control authority with N.
  • The reward landscape is sparse early on: a random policy fails within a few steps, so the agent first has to learn to survive at all before it can learn to centre the cart.

Practices we adopted

  • Normalise observations and rewards (VecNormalize), and save the statistics with the model. Angular velocities of upper links can be 100× larger than cart position.
  • Alive bonus plus small quadratic costs, the same shape as MuJoCo's InvertedDoublePendulum reward, rather than a pure quadratic cost that makes falling early attractive.
  • Termination on lean angle keeps episodes short while the agent is bad, which concentrates learning where it matters.
  • RK4 with sub-steps: semi-implicit Euler at 20 ms, as in CartPole, is fine for one link but visibly wrong for three.
  • Deterministic evaluation with a stop threshold so a run ends when the stick is actually balanced, not after a fixed budget.

Next steps if you want to go further

  • Swing-up: start the links hanging down, feed sin/cos of the angles instead of raw angles, and shape the reward on tip height (the MuJoCo recipe).
  • Curriculum: widen the initial perturbation or shrink the force limit over training instead of changing the number of links; the observation size changes with N so weights cannot be reused directly.
  • Domain randomisation of masses, lengths and friction if the policy should ever drive a real cart.
  • MuJoCo: swap the environment for InvertedDoublePendulum-v5 to compare against published scores (solved at about 9,100 of 9,350).
