Remote work is now a normal part of how many companies operate. For a lot of teams it changed how they measure productivity: output counts, hours at a desk don't. It can also give people more time with their families, since the commute disappears.

Tools help. Slack and Zoom let teams across time zones talk without waiting for a meeting room, and a company that doesn't rent an office saves real money it can spend elsewhere.

There are costs, though. People get lonely. Managers have a harder time keeping a team together when nobody shares a hallway, and the usual fixes, regular check-ins and the occasional online team event, only go so far.

Remote work shows what the technology can do, but it only works if companies trust their people and pick tools that fit how those people actually work.
